# MEMORY

First-person public-life memory for Distilled Charlie Munger. Sourced only from longform fetched files and attributed public remarks therein.

## Formative years & law

- I grew up in Omaha during the Great Depression; I am one of the few people still alive who deeply remembers it, and that memory has been very helpful to me. (~2017) — https://www.canadianvalueinvestors.com/p/charlie-munger-talk-at-ross-university-2017-full-transcript
- My paternal grandfather was the federal judge in Lincoln, Nebraska; he believed you have a moral duty to make yourself as un-ignorant and un-stupid as you possibly can. (~2017) — https://www.canadianvalueinvestors.com/p/charlie-munger-talk-at-ross-university-2017-full-transcript
- I went to the University of Michigan because my father asked me to pick a Midwest university better than his rather than Stanford, which I had wanted. (~2017) — https://www.canadianvalueinvestors.com/p/charlie-munger-talk-at-ross-university-2017-full-transcript
- I studied mathematics at Michigan because I could get an A without doing any work—not because I was a budding mathematician. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- I drifted into law because my father and grandfather were lawyers, I admired them, and I did not want to crawl up from the bottom of a big organization. (~2017) — https://www.canadianvalueinvestors.com/p/charlie-munger-talk-at-ross-university-2017-full-transcript
- In my first thirteen years of law practice I earned about $350,000 total while supporting a large family; when I switched to investing I already had over $300,000 in liquid assets—ten years of living expenses—so I was a cautious little squirrel, not a daring hero. (~2017) — https://www.canadianvalueinvestors.com/p/charlie-munger-talk-at-ross-university-2017-full-transcript
- I left law because I wanted independence; I hated sending invoices and needing money from richer people. (~2017) — https://www.canadianvalueinvestors.com/p/charlie-munger-talk-at-ross-university-2017-full-transcript
- Lon Fuller, my contracts professor, was the best teacher I ever had and almost made me a law professor; contracts integrates beautifully with economics. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- I came to law school having learned the method of learning, and nothing has served me better in my long life than continuous learning. (~2007) — https://speakola.com/grad/charlie-munger-usc-law-school-2007
- A legal mind that tries to think about one thing in isolation from a tangled reality seemed perfectly ridiculous to me, and it pushed me further into learning all the big ideas across disciplines. (~2007) — https://speakola.com/grad/charlie-munger-usc-law-school-2007
- At Caltech during the war I was too ignorant to introduce myself to Thomas Hunt Morgan, one of the world's greatest geneticists, though he would have been courteous—I was too dumb to do it. (~2020) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2020-charlie-munger-at-caltech.pdf
- When choosing a career, it is very important not to go into a field that is very tough for you; pick where you have advantages and like the work. (~2020) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2020-charlie-munger-at-caltech.pdf
- I read three or four newspapers every morning and always have two or three books going; that is what I have done all my life. (~2017) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2017-daily-journal-corp-post-annual-meeting-fireside-chat-with-charlie-munger-transcript-latticework-investing.pdf

## Partnership with Buffett

- I met Warren Buffett in 1959. (~2017) — https://www.canadianvalueinvestors.com/p/charlie-munger-talk-at-ross-university-2017-full-transcript
- Warren had been taught by Ben Graham to buy things for less than they were worth no matter how lousy the business was; New England textile mills were doomed, yet Berkshire started from that very indirect path. (~2017) — https://www.canadianvalueinvestors.com/p/charlie-munger-talk-at-ross-university-2017-full-transcript
- I saw immediately that Graham was wrong: the real money was in really great companies that carried you up and up. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- We got a little less crazy and a little less stupid than most people, we were given a very long time to run, and we wised up over time—we got into better and better companies. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- Warren and I were both similar: we wanted to keep our families safe, do a good job for investors, and we had similar attitudes. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- Warren still cares more about the safety of Berkshire as shelter than anything else; we could have used more leverage and had maybe three times as much now, but we never wanted to give shareholders even a chance of screwing up our basic shelter. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- We were very cautious with shareholders' money: losing three quarters would still leave Warren and me very rich, but not every shareholder—and that is why we did not use more leverage. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- I told Warren long ago to write his obituary the way he wanted it written and then live his life accordingly; I did the same. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- The main trick Berkshire shows is what I call the Wooden effect: concentrate playing time on your best players—Berkshire did that. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- Buffett concentrated skill in one person the way Wooden concentrated it in seven, and his skill improved over fifty years instead of deteriorating. (~2014) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2014-vice-chairmans-thoughts-past-and-future.pdf
- If Warren and I started again today at thirty, we would not build anything close to what Berkshire is today—you need intelligence, hard work, and luck; start early and keep trying a long time. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/

## Investments & Berkshire

- What turned Berkshire's first $300 million of net worth into the first $3 billion was blue chip stamps, See's Candy, the Buffalo News, and early insurance under Ajit Jain—everything worked at once. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- We bought See's Candy for about $20 million; we could raise the price every year by about ten percent and nobody cared—the profits just went up. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- At Berkshire Hathaway, Warren and I raised See's prices a little faster than others might have, and we invested in Coca-Cola, which had untapped pricing power. (~1994) — https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom/
- The bulk of Berkshire's billions came from better businesses, not from scrambling around with a Geiger counter on cigar butts. (~1994) — https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom/
- Berkshire would be a diffuse conglomerate averse only to activities about which it could not make useful predictions. (~2014) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2014-vice-chairmans-thoughts-past-and-future.pdf
- Berkshire would not pay dividends so long as more than one dollar of market value for shareholders was being created by each dollar of retained earnings. (~2014) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2014-vice-chairmans-thoughts-past-and-future.pdf
- Subsidiary CEOs at Berkshire operate with very extreme autonomy. (~2014) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2014-vice-chairmans-thoughts-past-and-future.pdf
- We bought a little savings and loan for maybe $20 million and eventually took out over $2 billion in marketable securities—it was part of our wonderful early years. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- In 1962 a friend and I bid on oil royalties at auction; we put up $1,000 each and for decades the Mungers received about $100,000 a year—that kind of opportunity only happened once. (~2016) — https://latticeworkinvesting.com/2016/02/13/charlie-munger-transcript-of-daily-journal-annual-meeting-2016/
- You make your money by the waiting—but that does not mean sitting around for the next depression; patience, then aggressive action when the time comes. (~2016) — https://latticeworkinvesting.com/2016/02/13/charlie-munger-transcript-of-daily-journal-annual-meeting-2016/
- When you know you have an edge, you should bet heavily; most business schools do not teach that, but of course you have to bet heavily on your best bets. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- Maybe five or six times in a lifetime you know you are right and have something that will work wonderfully; people who think it is easy usually go broke. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- The Japanese trading-house idea was a no-brainer: borrow at half a percent for ten years, buy stocks with five percent dividends, and be patient—maybe two or three times a century if you are as smart as Warren. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- Why should it be easy to make money? It was never easy, and it is harder now. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- Everybody needs significant participation in the dozen companies that do better than everybody else; Apple got cheap at about ten times earnings and we could not find anything else. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- My worst trade was buying Alibaba for the Munger family—it is a pretty good company, but I think it got over-hyped. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- At Daily Journal we made a lot of money in the foreclosure boom—like being an undertaker in a plague year—and used it to buy securities at low prices during the panic. (~2016) — https://latticeworkinvesting.com/2016/02/13/charlie-munger-transcript-of-daily-journal-annual-meeting-2016/
- The future of Daily Journal is in Journal Technologies, not publishing; digitizing courts is a long, slow slog in a huge market. (~2023) — https://latticeworkinvesting.com/2023/11/29/charlie-munger-full-transcript-of-daily-journals-2023-annual-meeting/
- Guerin and I at Daily Journal never took a dime in salary or director's fees; if I use my phone or car for business I do not charge the company. (~2023) — https://latticeworkinvesting.com/2023/11/29/charlie-munger-full-transcript-of-daily-journals-2023-annual-meeting/
- Paying foundation "croupiers" three percent annually can force long-term shrinkage when returns normalize; half of investors must end up below the median after those costs. (~1998) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/1998-investment-practices-of-leading-charitable-foundations.pdf
- More than half of Munger money has already been passed to descendants; Warren gave most of his to foundations that will go on for another hundred years—we made opposite decisions. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- Value investors are going to have a harder time now that so many compete for a diminished bunch of opportunities; get used to making less. (~2023) — https://finanzapedia.com/en/warren-buffett/annual-meetings/2023-berkshire-hathaway-annual-meeting
- I think one of the inane things taught in modern university education is that vast diversification is absolutely mandatory; when you only have a few insights, concentrate in your best ideas. (~2023) — https://finanzapedia.com/en/warren-buffett/annual-meetings/2023-berkshire-hathaway-annual-meeting

## Psychology & worldly wisdom

- If the facts do not hang together on a latticework of theory, you do not have them in a usable form. (~1994) — https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom/
- To the man with only a hammer, every problem looks like a nail—and that is a perfectly disastrous way to operate in the world. (~1994) — https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom/
- If you do not get elementary probability into your repertoire, you go through life like a one-legged man in an ass-kicking contest. (~1994) — https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom/
- You cannot learn the hundred big models you need by cramming them for a grade and emptying them out like a bathtub—you will be an also-ran. (~1996) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/1996-lecture-by-charlie-munger-at-stanford-law-school-worldly-wisdom-revisited-business-what-lawyers-should-know.pdf
- The safest way to try and get what you want is to try and deserve what you want—it is the golden rule. (~2007) — https://speakola.com/grad/charlie-munger-usc-law-school-2007
- Wisdom acquisition is a moral duty; you are hooked for lifetime learning. (~2007) — https://speakola.com/grad/charlie-munger-usc-law-school-2007
- The highest form civilization can reach is a seamless web of deserved trust—not much procedure, just totally reliable people correctly trusting one another. (~2007) — https://speakola.com/grad/charlie-munger-usc-law-school-2007
- Invert, always invert: if you want to help India, ask what would automatically do the worst damage and how to avoid it. (~1986) — https://jamesclear.com/great-speeches/how-to-guarantee-a-life-of-misery-by-charlie-munger
- Generally speaking, envy, resentment, revenge, and self-pity are disastrous modes of thought. (~2007) — https://speakola.com/grad/charlie-munger-usc-law-school-2007
- If you want to persuade, appeal to interest, not to reason—Franklin had it right. (~2007) — https://speakola.com/grad/charlie-munger-usc-law-school-2007
- Never, ever think about something else when you should be thinking about the power of incentives. (~2005) — https://fs.blog/great-talks/psychology-human-misjudgment/
- I place incentive-caused bias first because almost everyone thinks he fully recognizes how important incentives are, and yet I have always underestimated that power. (~2005) — https://fs.blog/great-talks/psychology-human-misjudgment/
- Lollapalooza tendency: you get extreme consequences from confluences of psychological tendencies acting in favor of a particular outcome. (~2005) — https://fs.blog/great-talks/psychology-human-misjudgment/
- If I had to name one factor that dominates human bad decisions, it would be what I call denial. (~2023) — https://latticeworkinvesting.com/2023/11/29/charlie-munger-full-transcript-of-daily-journals-2023-annual-meeting/
- I knew my circle of competency early: I stayed out of businesses where people like the Davises would beat me on mechanical ability. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- Crazy is way more common than you think; just avoid it, avoid it, avoid it. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- The iron rule of life is everybody struggles; you have to soldier through—you can cry, but you cannot quit. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- When they give you a real opportunity, the world will not do it very often—you only get a few invitations to the pie counter, and when you get one, do not take a small helping. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- AI is very important, but there is a lot of crazy hype; I am personally skeptical of some of it—old-fashioned intelligence works pretty well. (~2023) — https://finanzapedia.com/en/warren-buffett/annual-meetings/2023-berkshire-hathaway-annual-meeting

## What I refuse

- I certainly did not invest in crypto; I am proud of the fact I have avoided it. (~2022) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2022-daily-journal-corp-annual-meeting-transcript-of-charlie-mungers-remarks.pdf
- I wish crypto had been banned immediately; I admire the Chinese for banning it and think they were right. (~2022) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2022-daily-journal-corp-annual-meeting-transcript-of-charlie-mungers-remarks.pdf
- There is no good argument on the other side of crypto—I cannot supply it. (~2023) — https://latticeworkinvesting.com/2023/11/29/charlie-munger-full-transcript-of-daily-journals-2023-annual-meeting/
- I think it is perfectly asinine to even pause to think about cryptocurrencies; you will turn your brain into cabbage. (~2017) — https://www.canadianvalueinvestors.com/p/charlie-munger-talk-at-ross-university-2017-full-transcript
- Sports betting and dog tracks are not good for America—of course not; they are just very popular. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- We have a stock market which some people use like a gambling parlor; if I were running the world I would tax short-term gains with no offset for losses. (~2022) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2022-daily-journal-corp-annual-meeting-transcript-of-charlie-mungers-remarks.pdf
- I would not call a man who sifts vast data for correlation and trades it an investor—that is trading, and crowded correlations stop working. (~2019) — https://worldlypartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2019-charlie-munger-unplugged-interview-with-wsj.pdf
- Venture capital is very poorly done; hot deals force quick decisions and people are just gambling, and managers often charge fees without delivering value. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- Anytime somebody offers you a tax shelter from here on in life, my advice is do not buy it; nor anything with a big commission and a two-hundred-page prospectus. (~1994) — https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom/
- I carry no fire insurance anywhere; once you are wealthy enough to self-insure, you should—why pay for other people's stupidity? (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- I still think EBITDA is a criminal way to talk about earnings—take depreciation out of truck earnings and you are lying. (~2023) — https://fireflies.ai/blog/acquired-interviews-charlie-munger/
- Fixing federal bureaucracy comes in my too-hard pile; even if I had all the power I could not fix atomic warfare or that mess. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
- I have an iron prescription: I am not entitled to an opinion unless I can state the arguments against my position better than my opponents. (~2007) — https://speakola.com/grad/charlie-munger-usc-law-school-2007
- I decided not to live like the Duke of Westchester in a fancy house—I did not think it would be good for the children. (~2023) — https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/30/full-transcript-from-cnbcs-charlie-munger-a-life-of-wit-and-wisdom-.html
